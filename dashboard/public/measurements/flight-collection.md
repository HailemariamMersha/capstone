# Automatic trip collection: ASN-only qualification

**Current 1.3.0:** new trips use [paper-core-v1](paper-suite.md), with five trace targets, five CDN providers and resolver discovery. Controlled pairs described below are historical; they are not scheduled in new trips. ASN-only admission and upload lifecycle remain.

Current detector: `asn-only-v1` (private pilot 1.2.3), with explicit test-ASN provenance. Pilot budget policy is `flight-pilot-hourly-2026-11-20`; consent is `skymeasure-flight-payment-2026-11-20`. See [the complete pilot change and compatibility guide](https://github.com/HailemariamMersha/capstone/blob/feature/skymeasure-participant-dashboard/docs/skymeasure/uncapped-pilot.md). The detector version is separate from the allowance and consent versions. Historical records keep their original meaning.

## What starts collection

Start trip arms monitoring on the selected, process-bound Android Wi-Fi network. Flight number, departure and arrival are required participant reports, not verified flight information. A required NYU NetID is kept in a separate administrator-only payment mapping and never in measurement exports. New trips do not ask for or collect an expected duration. Historical fields remain readable.

Only a fresh provider ASN match can qualify a connection. The configured registry is Starlink AS14593, Inmarsat AS31515, Intelsat AS22351, Panasonic AS64294, SITA AS206433 and ViaSat AS40306. This is an incomplete provider allowlist, not an authoritative list of aircraft networks. A positive match means **provider-matched connectivity**, including possible ground use; it does not establish that a phone is airborne.

The server observes the request's public address through its trusted proxy and looks up GeoLite2-ASN. The phone refreshes identity approximately every minute, expires observations after two minutes, and invalidates them when the selected Wi-Fi handle changes. Lookup timeout is ten seconds, with a fifteen-second failure retry. Lookup failure is unknown, not a nonmatching ASN. ASN must be a positive 32-bit integer; invalid values are unknown. VPN attribution is unsupported; the app pauses rather than bypassing an active VPN.

The control lane evaluates every thirty seconds and on network changes. Two positive evaluations at least thirty seconds apart admit collection on that same Wi-Fi handle. Each observation records ASN state, reason, observation time, `confidence=asn_matched` and `detectorPolicyVersion=asn-only-v1`. Confidence describes the current evidence, not whether the trip ever qualified. A newly enrolled installation needs connectivity; an existing identity can arm monitoring offline, but cannot initially qualify without ASN evidence.

The private pilot additionally accepts AS5384 (TEST Etisalat) only when its build/configuration gates are enabled. It uses the same two-evaluation admission path, without researcher roles or bypass. Any such observation permanently marks the trip as ground-test evidence; dashboard flight totals exclude it by default. ASN identifies a whole provider network, not one Wi-Fi router.

SSID is optional context and cannot qualify a connection. Automatic trips no longer request GPS fixes or require location permission. Android location permission and enabled system Location can still be needed for optional Wi-Fi identifiers; missing identifiers remain null and do not prevent binding. The geolocation library/patch remains for legacy diagnostic code and historical interpretation, not automatic trip qualification.

## Pausing and ending

After qualification, an unknown/stale ASN on the **same** selected Wi-Fi retains packet attempts so outages remain observable. These periods are explicitly unconfirmed. A fresh nonmatching ASN clears qualification and pauses collection. A different Wi-Fi handle, even with the same SSID, requires qualification again. Captive portals remain connectivity limitations. Wi-Fi binding must be validated separately per protocol; simulation/unit tests alone cannot establish physical routing.

The ten-minute disconnection grace starts only after first qualification and covers loss of qualifying connectivity. Watching at home does not end after ten minutes. Stop, battery at or below 15% while not charging, or the 24-hour safety ceiling also end collection. GPS speed no longer ends an ASN-only trip. No ending claims a verified landing. Reopening after process loss preserves interrupted evidence and requires Start for new collection; force-stop/reboot does not promise automatic rearming.

The new phone interface exposes neither shadow mode nor manual researcher controls. Their old API authorization remains for historical clients; the current Etisalat pilot exercises normal admission and retains explicit ground-test labels.

## Schedule and allowance

Measurements remain serial: ten ICMP probes, twenty 128-byte UDP probes and one TCP connection approximately every minute; a twenty-hop, three-probe-per-hop traceroute first after one minute and then every fifteen minutes. The independent control lane continues detection and snapshots during long measurements. Actual execution and callback times, planned deadlines, gaps, cancellations and segment health remain observable; this is not a guaranteed wall-clock cadence. See [collector reliability](https://github.com/HailemariamMersha/capstone/blob/feature/skymeasure-participant-dashboard/docs/skymeasure/collector-reliability.md).

Historical pilot policy `flight-pilot-delivery-2026-11-23` uses a decimal 500,000-byte controlled download with a 45-second total ceiling and a 1,000,000-byte upload. Historical policies retain their recorded 5,000,000-byte download and timeout. First pair: five minutes after qualification; later spacing: one hour under `transferSchedule=hourly-v1`. Historical trips retain max(five minutes, expected length / ten), with a twenty-four-hour default. At most ten pair reservations. Late opportunities are skipped, with no catch-up burst. Failed/cancelled reservations are not refunded; two consecutive failures disable that controlled direction. The ten-pair maximum can leave late-flight controlled transfers unavailable on long flights. These short transfers are not Ookla or a comprehensive link-capacity test.

One automatic M-Lab NDT7 pair is scheduled with a saved one-to-three-minute delay after qualification, waiting for validated selected Wi-Fi. The private pilot has no byte cutoff and does not reserve 200 MiB. Normal protocol duration, the 60-second pair watchdog, Stop, one pair per trip and four attempts per 24 hours remain. Completed speed still requires valid clean completion; interrupted/failed runs retain evidence with `value=null`. Controlled transfers are spaced away from M-Lab's actual finish. See [the current uncapped pilot](https://github.com/HailemariamMersha/capstone/blob/feature/skymeasure-participant-dashboard/docs/skymeasure/uncapped-pilot.md) for payload-counter semantics and public-data disclosure.

The pilot keeps independent application-accounted allocations of 60 MiB CDN content (historical policies: controlled transfers), 20 MiB probes/context and 20 MiB live uploads, while M-Lab is outside those limits. There is no overall 300 MiB ceiling. Segment boundaries never reset counters. Historical 300 MiB policies retain their original 200 MiB reservation and byte caps. Exhausting a bounded allocation disables only that activity. Identity requests reserve 2048 bytes; new-policy live uploads reserve encoded request body bytes plus 64 KiB before sending, then charge encoded request bytes plus observed response text bytes when a response is known. Uncertain dispatched failures retain the full reservation; a proven pre-dispatch failure releases its hold. Outstanding holds survive process loss and are conservatively charged on recovery. Spent counters never decrease. Old policies retain their original request-plus-64-KiB accounting. These are not carrier-billed traffic: transport headers, retransmissions, authentication and post-trip uploads are additional/unobserved traffic.

The fourteen-hour probe payload estimate is 840 × (10×64×2 + 20×128×2) + 56×(20×3×32×2) = 5,591,040 bytes = 5.33 MiB. It excludes detection, context, headers, retransmissions and uploads; it is a model, not a measured interface counter. Loaded-latency tests are off for automatic trips.

## Trips, segments and exports

One trip contains ordered closed measurement sessions. Every fifteen minutes, stop admitting measurements. ICMP, UDP, TCP and traceroute receive at most ten seconds of grace from the scheduled boundary; CDN fetches, historical controlled transfers and M-Lab receive immediate cancellation. Save the settled result before closing. Then attempt uploads for up to twenty-five seconds before opening the next segment. Boundaries remain anchored to the original schedule, without accumulated drift. `segment_drain` and `upload_pause` separate grace from upload time. The foreground service and detector remain active. Qualification, budgets and transfer deadlines belong to the trip. `upload_pause` and scheduling events expose intentional gaps. A proposed scheduler using dedicated measurement blocks is not implemented here.

Trip revisions are monotonic; a closed trip declares its expected segment count. Upload completeness requires reconciliation of all those segments. Collection completion, upload completeness and successful measurements are independent. Received state is only the phone's last report, not a live presence indicator. Interrupted records remain valid partial evidence. Segments with no measurements may still contain detector evidence.

Raw JSON preserves configuration, original library responses, snapshots and events. New manifests carry `detectorPolicyVersion`; combined CSV carries `session.detectorPolicyVersion` and the observation's `measurement.networkSnapshot.flightEvidence.detectorPolicyVersion`. Historical records are not relabelled. Their `network_only` and `gps_supported` tiers refer to the former two-of-three detector. Historical derived altitude is WGS84 ellipsoid altitude, not sea-level altitude. Repeated context is not a fresh per-packet reading. Sum parents by unique measurement ID and allowances by trip ID, never by repeated sample rows. Missing values are unavailable, not zero. See the [dictionary](columns.md).

## Limits of the evidence

ASN matching cannot establish a flight, airline, exclusive provider or aircraft movement. Provider registry coverage needs real-flight validation. A Starlink reverse-DNS/PoP change is an observed egress-name change, not a proven satellite or gateway handover. No coordinates or raw location objects may enter persisted/exported records. App/library callbacks are not kernel timestamps or complete packet capture. Ground shadow tests and synthetic signals test software behavior, not detection accuracy or comparative reliability.

## ASN service limits and retry behavior

`/api/v1/context` uses the server's local GeoLite2-ASN MMDB, not a paid per-request WHOIS or MaxMind web API. The daily updater checks/downloads the database independently of participant requests. GeoLite accounts allow 30 database downloads per 24 hours; checks for updates do not count ([MaxMind](https://support.maxmind.com/knowledge-base/articles/download-and-update-maxmind-databases), checked 20 November 2026). The current API middleware limits all endpoints together to 300 requests per transport source IP per minute per process. Participants behind one cabin NAT share that limit. HTTP 429, timeout, stale/missing database and unavailable ASN remain unknown evidence, with a recorded reason; none is a provider non-match.

Post-trip uploads retry without button presses after Stop, app open/resume, eligible connectivity restoration and Android background opportunities. After Stop, the running foreground service switches in place to `dataSync` and drains successive 180-second passes, including with the screen locked or the app backgrounded. It stops when final receipts reconcile, authentication requires intervention, or ten minutes elapse without newly acknowledged records/completion progress. Android expiration is handled without clearing evidence. App-open retries may start the same upload service while visible. Headless OS opportunities retain a 25-second bound and do not join/cancel an extended service-owned pass. Record/session backoff survives restart; a separate owner-scoped backoff covers manifest/network failures. Collection and uploads remain mutually exclusive. Android background opportunities are not exact timers; force-stopped apps require reopening. The Retry uploads button remains available and uses the same ownership, preferences and acknowledgement checks.

## Interruptions and delivery evidence

Derived parent outcomes are `success`, `failure`, `interrupted`, and `unavailable`. `cancelled`/`interrupted` errors are interruptions, not network failures. New records carry an explicit `interruptionReason`; historical reasons remain unknown. Partial valid samples stay in the export. Unsent/cancelled probes do not enter packet-loss denominators. Raw `success` and error fields remain immutable.

Upload bodies may use gzip; actual probe download/upload bodies remain uncompressed. New servers advertise `X-SkyMeasure-Sync: 2`; clients then use up to 200 records with a 512 KiB decoded-body target. Accepted immutable session manifests are cached by owner, API environment and fingerprint; changed trip state still needs registration. A compact trip receipt confirms the final accepted revision and segment completeness. The bounded local request journal is operational evidence, separate from closed segments.
